Laue symmetry overview

How to read these plots

Intensity-weighted reciprocal-lattice planes (true metric: a*, b*, c* and their angles). Each circle is the mean intensity at one lattice point, averaged only over exact repeats — never over symmetry equivalents, so the symmetry of the pattern is the measured one. Circles are scaled to a shared reference (99th percentile of positive intensities) so the three zones are comparable; stronger spots are capped. Tiny dots: I ≤ 0.

Layer viewer

How to read this

Page through the reciprocal lattice layer by layer (hk0, hk1, hk2 … or h0l, h1l … or 0kl, 1kl …), drawn in the true reciprocal metric like the plots above. Circles = mean intensity per lattice point (same scale and size setting as above). Red rings mark gaps: positions that the lattice centering allows and that lie inside the measured resolution range, but that were not measured — and neither was any symmetry equivalent under the Laue class used here. A reflection that is missing but whose symmetry equivalent was measured is not a gap. Positions that the absence scan found to be systematically forbidden are not marked. Beamstop shadows, detector gaps and short scans show up as clusters of rings. Optional marks (off by default): ◎ outliers — an observation that disagrees with the other observations of its symmetry class (|I − ⟨I⟩| / σ above the chosen threshold; needs unmerged data), typical for a spot of a second domain on top of a reflection, the beamstop, a detector edge or a bad frame; ✕ forbidden but observed — systematically absent under the observed lattice and the absence conditions that held, yet I/σ ≥ 3. Hover over a reflection for its intensity, its symmetry class and batches; click to pin that information. ⤢ enlarges the picture together with the controls.
The 3D view on the right shows the whole reciprocal lattice inside the resolution sphere, with the current layer as a plane through it and the same marks. Drag to rotate, wheel or pinch to zoom, double-click to reset; ⤢ enlarges it together with its controls.

Twinning

Twin laws beyond the Laue class: if the lattice has (pseudo-)symmetry beyond the Laue class — 2-fold axes searched independent of the cell choice (Le Page) — the crystal can be twinned by the extra operations; the H test (Yeates 1988) compares twin-related reflection pairs and estimates the twin fraction. Inversion (racemic) twinning: compares the Bijvoet pairs I(h)/I(−h) with the anomalous signal expected from the composition. Nothing is written to the .ins unless you tick it below.

Load reflection data and a cell to run the twin checks.

Zone plots (reciprocal lattice)

How to read the zone plots

Drawn in the true reciprocal lattice (a*, b*, c* and their angles). Each square/dot is one aggregated lattice position (duplicate observations merged): ● filled green = at least one significant reflection measured there; ● light gray = measured but every observation was weak (I/σ < 3); blank = no reflection at that position at all. A clean checkerboard or every-other-row pattern is the visual signature of a glide plane / screw axis.
