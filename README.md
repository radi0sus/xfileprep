# XFilePrep

A browser-based tool for space-group determination from single-crystal diffraction data, with SHELX `.ins` file generation.

Runs entirely client-side — open `index.html` directly in a browser, no server or build step required.

## What it does

1. **File & Cell** — Load reflection data (SHELX `.hkl`, CIF, `.p4p`) or a `.ins` file; enter or edit unit cell parameters and the chemical formula, the wavelength (Mo/Cu/Ag Kα or any other value; taken from CIF/.ins/.p4p when they give one, otherwise Mo Kα is assumed and marked as such) and Z. A **Contents & crystal data** block shows the Z estimate (V / 18 Å³ per non-H atom), the composition used, formula weight, calculated density, F(000), Z′ for the selected space group, the linear absorption coefficient μ and the f″ of each element; with a crystal size (typed in, or read from the CIF `_exptl_crystal_size_max/_mid/_min` or a SHELX `SIZE` line in a .ins/.res; written back as `SIZE dmin dmid dmax` into the generated .ins when all three are known) also μ·r (min/max) with a rough rating and the expected T<sub>min</sub>/T<sub>max</sub>. λ and Z feed the `CELL`, `ZERR` and `UNIT` lines of the .ins and the composition for the E² statistics.
2. **Laue Symmetry** — Intensity-weighted reciprocal-lattice planes (0kl, h0l, hk0) drawn with the true reciprocal metric; circle size follows the mean intensity per lattice point (averaged over exact repeats only, never over symmetry equivalents). A **layer viewer** pages through the reciprocal layers hk*L*, h*K*l and *H*kl with a slider (or ▶ Play); red rings mark gaps — positions the lattice allows that were neither measured nor covered by a symmetry equivalent. An optional checkbox **fill in symmetry equivalents** (off by default) completes the zone plots and the layer viewer for merged data: positions that were not measured themselves get the mean intensity of their measured equivalents under the Laue class and are drawn in orange; measured positions, the intensity scale and the gaps are unchanged. It shows the assumed symmetry, so it is meant for orientation, not for judging the Laue class. Below that, a table of **merging statistics per resolution shell** (shells of equal d*³ width): unique reflections, completeness, redundancy, ⟨I/σ⟩, I > 2σ, CC½, Rint, Rmeas and Rpim, plus curves of the main quantities — for orientation only, nothing is cut or written to the .ins/.hkl.
3. **Twinning** — Its own tab, because twinning is easy to overlook: when something is found, the tab shows ⚠, a banner under the tab bar names it on every tab, and the .ins tab warns as long as it is not written to the .ins. First the **lattice symmetry**: 2-fold axes found with a Le Page search, independent of the cell choice, listed with their obliquity δ and whether they belong to the Laue class. If the lattice has higher (pseudo-)symmetry than the Laue class — found with a Le Page search for 2-fold axes, independent of the cell choice (e.g. monoclinic with β ≈ 90°, Laue 4/m in a tetragonal lattice, or a C 2/c cell with β = 132.6° that is pseudo-orthorhombic in another setting) — every possible twin law is tested with the H test (Yeates 1988) — obliquity δ, number of acentric twin-related pairs, ⟨H⟩, estimated twin fraction α, verdict, plus a cumulative **N(H) plot** against the ideal lines for α = 0 … 0.4 (a curve bending away from them points to pseudo-symmetry). A checkbox (off by default) writes the strongest law as `TWIN` + `BASF` into the .ins. Inversion (racemic) twinning is checked from the **Bijvoet pairs**: merged under the point group of an acentric candidate, the noise-corrected differences I(h) − I(−h) are compared with the anomalous signal expected from the composition and the f″ values of the CIF (`_atom_type_scat_dispersion_imag`) or, without a CIF, of the built-in table for Mo/Cu/Ag Kα; their ratio ≈ |1 − 2x| gives an estimated inversion-twin fraction x (like the Flack parameter, but without a model and without sign — a 50:50 racemic twin looks like a centrosymmetric structure). A second checkbox writes `TWIN` + `BASF x`; together with a twin law above it becomes `TWIN … -4` with three BASF values. If twinning is found, the E² tab says so above its statistics: twinning narrows the intensity distribution, so its centrosymmetric/acentric verdict is unreliable.
4. **Absences** — Scans systematic absences (centering, axial, glide/zone conditions) and visualizes them as zone plots (true reciprocal metric) and axial strips. Click a zone plot or axial strip (here or in the Laue tab), the Wilson plot or the N(z) plot, to enlarge it over a blurred page; Esc or a click outside closes it.
5. **E² Statistics** — Wilson plot, ⟨|E²−1|⟩ against the centrosymmetric/acentric references and an N(z) test (centric, acentric and twinned-acentric curves). The same ⟨|E²−1|⟩ is also shown per resolution shell, using the same shell boundaries as the merging table on the Laue tab (with a warning if the shells disagree, e.g. because of a heavy atom or pseudosymmetry). Centering-absent reflections of the observed lattice are excluded first. Needs a composition (formula + Z, or a `.ins`).
6. **Space Group** — Matches candidate space groups against the observed absences; a centrosymmetric/acentric tie is resolved with the E² verdict from the previous tab. Below the candidate table, close runners-up that lose on a clear difference are explained, and a warning appears when the top candidate is not clearly separated from the next one.
7. **SHELX .ins and .hkl** — Generates a ready-to-use `.ins` file for the selected space group, kept in sync with the current cell, formula, and candidate selection. If the selected candidate isn't the one the as-measured cell/hkl natively fit, the cell is transformed into that setting and the corresponding matrix is written on the `HKLF` line (the `.hkl` file itself is never touched). If the reflections came from a CIF (a `_refln` loop or an embedded `_shelx_hkl_file` block) rather than from a raw SHELX `.hkl`, this tab also offers to save them as a SHELX `.hkl` — untransformed, same base name as the `.ins`.

```
    e-statistics.js     — Wilson plot / E² statistics / N(z) test / lattice filter
    shell-stats.js      — merging statistics per resolution shell (completeness, ⟨I/σ⟩, R values, CC½) and shell curves
    twin-viz.js         — cumulative N(H) plot for the H test
    twin-check.js       — lattice 2-folds (Le Page), (pseudo-)merohedral twin laws beyond the Laue class, H test, twin fraction
    anomalous-check.js  — Bijvoet-pair anomalous signal, estimated inversion-twin fraction, f″ from the CIF
    layer-viewer.js     — reciprocal-layer slider with gap markers
    ins-writer.js       — SHELX .ins generation
    hkl-writer.js       — SHELX .hkl export for reflections that came from a CIF
    plot-theme.js       — light/dark palette for canvas plots
    plot-zoom.js        — click-to-enlarge overlay for zone plots, axial strips and the Wilson plot
    tabs.js, app.js      — UI wiring
```

## Notes

- CIF space-group metadata (Hall symbol, or H-M name + IT number) are used as an independent source for the Laue class; if they contradict the cell metric, the app falls back to the data analysis. Rint stays visible as a separate control table.
- No build tools, bundlers, or external runtime dependencies — vanilla JavaScript throughout.
- Symmetry and systematic-absence logic is derived from Hall symbols and symmetry operators at runtime, not hardcoded per space group.
- Some ambiguities (e.g. centrosymmetric vs. acentric candidates with identical absences) are inherent to X-ray diffraction (Friedel's law) and cannot be resolved from absences alone — E² statistics is provided to help with those cases.
- Setting transformation covers: all monoclinic settings (cell choices 1/2/3, A/C/I centering, a-, b- and c-unique, incl. the -b1.. entries of Nos. 9/15) and orthorhombic-style axis permutation. Matrices are in SHELX/HKLF convention (a' = M a, h' = M h); the symmetry operators are checked with the coordinate matrix (M⁻¹)ᵀ — up to v2 they were conjugated with M itself, which is only correct for pure axis permutations, so every monoclinic shear (e.g. P2₁/n → P2₁/c, I2/a → C2/c) got a wrong HKLF matrix. For monoclinic groups all matrices with entries −1/0/1 are searched and the one giving the least oblique cell (shortest axes, angle closest to 90°, obtuse) is used; if the selected setting is still clearly more oblique than the measured one, the Space Group and .ins tabs say so (IUCr: least oblique cell choice is recommended). Origin choices 1/2 (e.g. F d -3 m) are recognised as a pure origin shift (no HKLF matrix, see below). Not covered: the hexagonal↔rhombohedral axes transformation for R-centered trigonal groups (a fractional, not integer, transform) — reported as "not found" rather than guessed at.
- Match scores are always computed against the raw, as-measured HKL indices — never against a transformed re-indexing. So a candidate's standard-setting sibling (e.g. Pbca vs. the top-scoring Pcab) can legitimately score far lower even though it describes the identical physical structure: the score reflects how well the *unmodified* indices happen to fit that candidate's own axis convention, not the structure's correctness. This is why the Space Group tab surfaces a top candidate's standard sibling even when its score is low — the low score is expected there, not a red flag.
- Completeness and the shell boundaries only count reflections as possible that the lattice centering and the systematic absences found by the absence scan (screw axes, glide planes) allow, so data already cleaned of absences (deposited CIF/.hkl files) are not rated incomplete. For CIF data, the Laue class used by all statistics (E², shells, layer viewer) comes from the CIF space group.
- Pseudo-centering (e.g. weak but present h+l-odd reflections of a primitive cell) is no longer read as a lattice absence: the "weak tail" rule of the absence scan only applies if the forbidden class is also weak relative to the allowed one (mean intensity < 10 %).
- `summarizeHKL` uses loops instead of `Math.min(...array)`: large files (more than about 65 000 reflections on iPad/iPhone, about 120 000 elsewhere) used to overflow the call stack.
- Candidate scoring: a candidate that predicts only the weaker axial condition (00l: l = 2n) is penalised if the data obey the stronger one (l = 4n, a 4₁ axis). Before, I 4 c m and I 4₁ c d scored the same. The Match column shows one decimal.
- The absence scan and the candidate scoring know the **d-glide** (quarter translation: a sum modulo 4, e.g. 0kl: k+l = 4n as in F d d 2) and the **diagonal plane hhl** (glide/mirror planes ⊥ [1-10] in tetragonal, ⊥ [210] in hexagonal/trigonal and {110} in cubic groups; e.g. 2h+l = 4n in I 4₁ c d and I a -3 d). The theoretical conditions are derived from the operators as the set of allowed index pairs (mod 12); a candidate "expects" a condition to hold if all its allowed pairs satisfy it, so a- and b-glide together, or a d-glide, also imply the n-condition. Neutral checks (a d/hhl condition that is violated while the candidate predicts nothing there) do not count, so the absolute match values do not drop. Not covered: rhombohedral axes, the second diagonal plane h-hl (equivalent under 3-/4-/6-fold symmetry), a dedicated hhl plot.
- Space Group tab: below the candidate table, a block explains why close runners-up lose on a **clear** difference (a condition the scan found clearly held or violated, where the candidate predicts the opposite). If a candidate is within 1.5 points of the top and **no** such difference separates them, a warning ("not clearly separated") appears; weak data, pseudosymmetry, a condition the scan does not cover and enantiomorphic pairs (P 4₁/P 4₃) are listed as possible causes.
- Shell table (Laue tab): the CC½ column uses the σ–τ method (Assmann, Brehm & Diederichs 2016, as in XDSCC12 and CrystFEL's partialator; see the XDSwiki page "CC1/2"): deterministic, no random split, independent of the σ values; orange below 30 %. Two curves below the table show completeness, I > 2σ, CC½ and Rmeas (percent) against (sin θ/λ)², and ⟨I/σ⟩ on a log axis with a line at 2. For merged data (redundancy 1), CC½ and the R values are "—".
- Screw axes along a **short axis**: an axial row needs only 3 allowed reflections (zones: 5). With b = 5.5 Å at 0.77 Å resolution 0k0 holds just 020/040/060, so the 2₁ was always "undetermined" and P 2/a, P a and P 2₁/a tied (COD 1543504, really P 2₁/a). The "why runners-up score lower" block also counts such an axial check as decisive, and says when the forbidden reflections are missing from the file altogether (deposited data with absences removed). Together with this, the "stricter period" check (I 4 c m vs. I 4₁ c d, see above) is now asked only among the reflections the predicted period allows (e.g. "of the k = 2n, are the k = 4n+2 absent?"); over the whole row the candidate's own absences diluted it, and P 2₁/n lost to P n / P 2/n.
- **Few allowed reflections, strong forbidden ones:** a condition with fewer allowed reflections than the minimum can't be confirmed, but it is now judged *violated* when at least two forbidden reflections are significant and reach ≥ 10 % of the allowed mean intensity (COD 1501632, I -4 2 m: 00l holds only 004/008 for l = 4n while 002/006 are strong, so I 4₁ 2 2 drops out instead of tying).
- **Identical extinction symbols:** candidates whose predicted absences are exactly the same (e.g. I 4 2 2, I 4 m m, I -4 m 2, I -4 2 m, I 4/m m m for "I - - -") are reported as indistinguishable by absences instead of "not clearly separated". If a loaded CIF reports one of the top-scoring candidates, that one is preselected (and kept by the E² step if it fits its verdict); otherwise the order stays as before.
- **F-centred data:** an F lattice satisfies the A, B and C conditions as well; the observed centring is now the most specific one that holds (F > I > R > A/B/C). Before, F data were taken as A-centred and e.g. m-3m had no candidates at all (COD 2023426).
- **Hall parser, N = 1:** "-1n", "-1ab", "-1bw" at the third position of origin-choice-1 symbols (Nos. 59, 125, 129, 130, 133, 137, 138, 141, 142) were read as -3 along [111]; they are the inversion with a translation. These settings had cubic rotation groups and never matched their Laue class.
- **Origin choices:** origin choice 1 vs 2 is found as a pure origin shift (shift search in 24ths — F d -3 m, F d d d, I 41/a m d differ by 1/8); shown as "matches as measured (other origin choice)", no HKLF matrix.
- **SYMM cards:** one per rotation, no bare centring vectors (I 41 c d: 7 instead of 15; F d -3 m:2: 23 instead of 95). The groups were correct before, only redundant.
- **Condition signature:** it was `JSON.stringify` of the derived conditions, in which the zone sets (`Set`) became `{}` — all zonal conditions of the full model, hhl in particular, were ignored. 50 pairs with different absences (P 4₂ 2 2 / P 4₂ m c, F 4 3 2 / F -4 3 c, P 6₃ 2 2 / P 6₃ m c, ...) looked identical, 6 identical ones (F d d d:1/:2, R 3 2 / R 3 m, ...) looked different. Affected the "identical absences" note, CIF preselection and setting deduplication — not the scores themselves. The signature is now exactly what the scoring tests.
- **Twinning check (H test):** twin laws are the coset representatives of the metric holohedry modulo the Laue group (written as a 2-fold along a cell axis where possible, c first, e.g. `TWIN -1 0 0 0 -1 0 0 0 1`). Pairs are selected by the sum, I₁ + I₂ > 10σ (3σ if fewer than 200 pairs): given the sum, the share of two independent intensities is uniform, so ⟨H⟩ stays unbiased — requiring both reflections to be strong gave α ≈ 0.2 for untwinned simulated data. α = ½ − ⟨H⟩ is overestimated by pseudo-symmetry (yw6, P 2₁, β = 90.07°, Z = 8: refined 0.29, H test 0.35) and lowered by noise; α ≥ 0.40 is indistinguishable from the higher Laue class (compare Rint). The L test was tried and left out: it gave the same values for untwinned and twinned small-molecule data. Inversion (racemic) twinning is invisible in the intensities; for acentric space groups the check says so. With a setting change, the TWIN matrix is expressed in the new indices.
- **Inversion twin from Bijvoet pairs:** σ is first rescaled by the scatter of repeated measurements within each Bijvoet class (data-reduction σ are often too small; uncorrected, a centrosymmetric structure showed χ² = 1.8 and looked acentric). Expected signal for randomly placed atoms: ⟨ΔI²⟩/⟨I²⟩ = 4 Σf″² / Σf₀²(s). Checked against refined values: jg6 (Cu, Flack −0.006) → 0.02, nm75_26 (Co, Flack 0.45) → 0.40, yw6 (Br/Co, inversion share 0.45) → 0.43, COD 1501632 (Sr/Cu, Flack 0.52, merged) → 0.48. f″ come from the CIF if one is loaded (those belong to the refinement); elements the CIF does not list — or all elements, without a CIF — are taken from the built-in NIST FFAST table for the wavelength selected on the File & Cell tab (Mo/Cu/Ag Kα), and the tab says which source was used. Only for other wavelengths without a CIF is just the observed signal shown. Checked with simulated P 2₁ data (Br, Mo Kα, f″ from the table): enantiopure → x = 0.01, 30 % inverted → x = 0.30.
- **Scoring of violated conditions:** the share of forbidden reflections above 3σ is now compared with the share of allowed reflections above 3σ. With weak data a fully violated glide shows only that share (nm75_26: hk0 n-glide 46 %, allowed class ~50 %), and the raw percentage made P n a n outscore P n a 2₁.
- **CIF before E²:** a space group the CIF reports and that ties at the top score is kept, also when it already is the top row; E² that votes otherwise is shown as a note instead of switching (pseudo-symmetry misleads E²: nm75_26 and yw6 read "centrosymmetric", both are acentric).
- **Lattice symmetry for the twin check (Le Page 1982):** 2-fold axes are lattice vectors t (|indices| ≤ 2, including centring vectors) nearly parallel to a reciprocal vector h, obliquity δ ≤ 3°; R = 2 t hᵀ/(t·h) − I must be integral and keep the centring. The group they generate (with the Laue group and −1) replaces the holohedry read from the cell angles, which missed pseudo-symmetry hidden in another cell choice (nm56_25, C 2/c, β = 132.6°: 2-fold along [001] ⊥ (−1 0 1), δ = 0.012°, exactly the refined `TWIN -1 0 -2 0 -1 0 0 0 1`; H test α = 0.34, refined BASF 0.37). Untwinned controls with lattice pseudo-symmetry (COD 1561021 pseudo-tetragonal δ = 0.44°, COD 1543504 a ≈ c δ = 0.06°) give α = 0.00.
- **Crystal data and absorption:** μ/ρ (total: photoelectric + coherent + incoherent) and f″ come from NIST FFAST (C. T. Chantler et al., *X-Ray Form Factor, Attenuation and Scattering Tables*, version 2.1, 2005, http://physics.nist.gov/ffast; J. Phys. Chem. Ref. Data 29, 597 (2000) and 24, 71 (1995)), interpolated log-log at the three anode wavelengths and stored in `absorption-data.js` by a one-off script, not typed by hand. Only Cu, Mo and Ag Kα are tabulated; for any other wavelength μ and f″ are not shown (density, F(000) and Z still are). f′ is not included — FFAST's f1 needs further corrections to become a crystallographic f′, and nothing here uses it. Atomic weights are IUPAC standard values, cross-checked against the masses implied by the FFAST data (all 92 within 0.3 %). F(000) is the electron count of the cell (no dispersion). T<sub>min</sub>/T<sub>max</sub> are the outer limits exp(−μ·d<sub>max</sub>) and exp(−μ·d<sub>min</sub>) (the same idea as checkCIF's T<sub>min</sub>′/T<sub>max</sub>) — for orientation, not a replacement for the values of the absorption correction. μ can differ from SHELXL's value by about 1 %: SHELXL uses the cross-sections of International Tables Vol. C (Table 4.2.4.2), this app NIST FFAST (checked with yw6: Mo Kα, Br/Co — 1.989 here, 2.005 in SHELXL). A wavelength from a file is written to the .ins unchanged (e.g. 1.54178) as long as the matching anode stays selected; an exact SFAC/UNIT composition from a loaded .ins is used until the formula or Z are edited by hand.
